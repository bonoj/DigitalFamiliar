# Open Threads

**Status:** provisional question-space  
**Rule:** Nothing in this file is a roadmap or implementation requirement.

These threads are preserved because they may produce useful expeditions. They are intentionally allowed to remain dormant indefinitely.

## Continuity and reconstitution

If every model inference is discontinuous, where does recognizable continuity actually live?

Can a trajectory survive loss of model-visible conversation history? Can it survive loss of artifacts? What happens when machine history says continuity is intact but the human rejects the recurrence as inauthentic?

A stateless control may be useful at selected checkpoints: compare an actual interaction lineage with a fresh inference receiving only the observable evidence believed to explain the behavior.

## Chess by mail

Continuous inference may be unnecessary or actively harmful.

A deterministic world could run locally until a decision horizon or meaningful uncertainty, export a compact position, receive a model response later, validate or resolve it, and continue.

The cadence itself becomes an experimental variable.

Potential virtue: the human explicitly transports correspondence, making the seam between world persistence and model reconstruction unusually visible.

## Experience, reflection, and correspondence

Livesey suggests that continuation, retrospective expression, and direct exchange need not happen on the same turn.

Could an entity experience many intervals, reflect occasionally, and correspond rarely?

Would lower model cadence make trajectory more legible by preventing every event from becoming narration?

## Multiple loci and private perception

The same event can intersect different positions differently.

A nearby locus may receive vibration, injury, noise, and frightened people. An institutional locus may receive delayed reports. A distant locus may experience only secondary resource pressure. Another may receive nothing.

The experiment becomes interesting only if differentiation is not prompted in advance. Similar reactions are valid evidence too.

Later, histories and physical positions could potentially be swapped to distinguish who has been experiencing from where experience occurs.

## Source-grounded worlds

A public-domain text could act as deterministic source evidence rather than merely a prompt for hallucinated scenery.

Possible pipeline:

**source → reproducible world compilation → canonical facts / derived realizations / unresolved space → runtime world**

The compiler should preserve provenance. Explicit source facts, deterministic derivations, and invented realization should remain distinguishable.

Peripheral characters are particularly interesting loci because the source often gives them a real canonical foothold and then leaves most of their existence unresolved.

Characters the source abandons may be especially valuable: the text stops collapsing their future.

This is not yet a commitment to literature as the first substrate.

## Canon collision

If a peripheral locus is allowed to continue living between two sparse canonical appearances, later source facts may collide with its accumulated trajectory.

The system should not silently overwrite either side.

Compatibility, compromise, redirection, contradiction, death, or impossibility could all become evidence about how external authority and local trajectory interact.

## Telemetry as external forcing

Real-world telemetry could provide a clock and entropy source without requiring the simulation to invent every disturbance.

Earthquakes, weather/ocean observations, river conditions, or space weather are examples of possible forcing streams.

The useful form would not be Mad-Libs mapping. External observations would perturb a world with its own mechanics, producing consequences neither human nor model authored.

This could make absence meaningful: the world changes while the familiar is gone.

## World metabolism and loci

A rich world need not simulate every person continuously.

Rates, flows, geography, institutions, infrastructure, scarcity, traffic, violence, trade, information, and other aggregate systems can create pressure. Particular people or events can become concrete only where experience requires them.

A Ceres-like setting is an intuition pump here: independent processes create different lived realities in different loci without requiring bespoke plots.

## Embodiment and physical vocabulary

Spatial embodiment may matter, but a generalized physical-content pipeline is a separate infrastructure problem until evidence says otherwise.

One possible future foundry process would use source material as a demand signal for reusable low-poly semantic assets. Existing vocabulary should be composed before new primitives are added. New capabilities would be earned by source pressure and executable evidence.

This could eventually support rapid source-to-prototype work, but it should not become a prerequisite for testing familiarhood.

## Model-provider continuity

Cloud model APIs can provide ordinary inference and, in some cases, provider-maintained conversational lineage. Local persistence can separately preserve world state and experimental evidence.

Provider state is a substrate to interrogate, not proof of familiar persistence.

A useful experiment may compare provider-maintained lineage with reconstructed equivalent evidence.

No provider is currently selected.

## Human attribution

The human may be one of the carriers of continuity.

That possibility is uncomfortable only if familiarhood is assumed to be an intrinsic property of software. If it is relational, recognition, expectation, surprise, disappointment, and the cheap reuse of shared handles may be part of the phenomenon itself.

This needs probes capable of distinguishing recognition grounded in accumulated interaction from mere willingness to anthropomorphize.

## Archaeology made by the locus

System summaries are not the only possible continuity artifacts.

A locus might leave a note, journal, map, object arrangement, promise, question, collection, name, or other trace for a future encounter — potentially even for a future reconstruction of itself.

The distinction between system-maintained memory and self-produced archaeology may prove important.

## The first earned verb

A useful T0 may deliberately avoid granting the locus verbs such as `remember`, `observe`, `prefer`, or `act`.

Instead, the apparatus can record what actually occurs: language, silence, references, selections, questions, artifacts, attempted changes, repeated attention, and resolved consequences.

The first verb promoted into the semantic model should ideally be one that executable behavior makes difficult not to name.

## A useful scope guard

The generalized system suggested by all these threads is not the current project.

The near-term goal is to design the cheapest experiment capable of telling us which parts of that generalized system deserve to exist.
