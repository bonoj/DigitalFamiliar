# Current Working Model

**Status:** provisional research synthesis  
**Date:** 2026-09-29

This note compresses the current investigation without promoting its hypotheses into project authority.

## The central possibility

A digital familiar may be less like a persistent software object and more like a **runtime trajectory** repeatedly reconstituted across discontinuous inference events.

For the model, each inference is in an important sense another first date. Whatever continuity exists must be made available again through some combination of conversation state, shared-world consequences, artifacts, previous language, model-visible records, and the human's own changed expectations.

This suggests a useful distinction:

> Do not assume we need to preserve the familiar's internal state. We may instead need to preserve enough evidence for a future inference to recover a recognizable trajectory.

This is a hypothesis, not a conclusion.

## Possible carriers of continuity

Current candidates include:

- model-visible conversational history or provider-maintained interaction lineage;
- deterministic or persistent world state;
- artifacts made during prior encounters;
- names, handles, commitments, questions, and compressed shared vocabulary;
- consequences of prior choices;
- bounded private experience;
- human memory, attribution, and recognition.

None individually has been established as the familiar. Their relative necessity is unknown.

Human attribution should not automatically be treated as contamination. If familiarhood is relational, the human's recognition of recurrence may be part of the phenomenon. Conversely, machine-accessible history can remain perfectly coherent while the human experiences a continuity failure.

## Three useful rhythms

Prior experiments suggest that model involvement need not happen at one cadence.

### Experience

A low-friction continuation can allow a locus to encounter another bounded interval without demanding dialogue, drama, or explicit reflection.

The Livesey experiment's simple continuation gesture is evidence that situated continuity can become visible without ordinary conversation.

### Reflection

At a slower cadence, a locus may externalize accumulated experience through a journal, map, sketch, collection, changed description, question, name, or some other artifact.

The important experimental possibility is not to tell it what to remember, but to observe what survives into later expression.

### Correspondence

At a still different cadence, another mind may become useful. A world can run until a semantically interesting boundary, package a bounded position, and exchange it with a model.

The current shorthand is **chess by mail**: inference need not occupy every simulation tick.

These rhythms are candidates, not required subsystems.

## Locus rather than character

A useful neutral term is **locus**: something whose trajectory can be followed from a bounded position in a world.

A locus need not initially have a name, personality, occupation, body, biography, goals, or even a conversational role. Those properties should not be introduced merely to make it feel alive.

Multiple loci around the same event may provide a strong probe. If different histories and apertures produce different subsequent behavior without personality sheets, that is interesting. If they do not, that is equally useful evidence.

## Independent world pressure

A trajectory may need something outside the model that continues to produce consequences.

Possible substrates include:

- a small authored deterministic simulation;
- a source-grounded world compiled from literature or another corpus;
- real-world telemetry used as external forcing;
- an embodied spatial apparatus;
- combinations of these.

The substrate should not be selected merely because it is rich. The first world should be only rich enough to put useful pressure on the hypothesis.

A promising separation is:

**world metabolism → bounded locus → experience → model-mediated interpretation/proposal/artifact/silence → external resolution → changed world**

The familiar machinery, if such machinery emerges, should not need to know what kind of world it inhabits.

## Reconstruction as an experimental target

If continuity is reconstitution rather than uninterrupted process, we can perturb its carriers.

Examples worth future testing include:

- actual conversation lineage versus a fresh model given reconstructed observable history;
- preserved artifacts with conversation lineage removed;
- preserved history with world consequences removed;
- loci moved between positions while retaining their histories;
- identical current observations after divergent private histories;
- long gaps between encounters;
- a human recognizing or rejecting continuity despite nominally equivalent machine context.

These are probes, not a testing program yet.

## Strongest current constraint

Do not ask a familiar to perform continuity.

Give a model-mediated locus somewhere continuity could become visible, then inspect what actually persists, recurs, changes, or fails.


## New executable pressure: trajectory across expeditions

Astra supplies a concrete neighboring case that the earlier synthesis did not yet have.

Astra's persistent surface is not one world, one conversation, or one executable. The observable unit is a sequence of model-operated expeditions. Project repositories preserve the local work and its consequences. Astra's own repository can preserve whatever cross-project record later runs judge useful. A later discontinuous run can orient from those surfaces and enter new work without requiring the human to reconstruct the entire prior trajectory.

This suggests three continuity surfaces that should remain analytically separate:

**actor trajectory** — enough survives for later model-operated work to recover and extend a path across expeditions;

**work trajectory** — project repositories preserve local authority, evidence, artifacts, failures, and consequences independently of any one model inference;

**encounter trajectory** — a crossing can preserve enough shared situation for an independently instantiated model conversation to continue participating coherently.

AstralBridge's first real run supplies evidence for the third surface. Ten manual crossings through one continuing Google AI Mode conversation accumulated consequences across sparse prompts, including a bare continuation gesture. The run also showed that cognitive continuity can be cheap while mechanical transport remains expensive.

Astra and AstralBridge do not prove that trajectory is familiarhood. They make trajectory experimentally available without requiring us to first invent a familiar-shaped application.

A useful sharpened hypothesis is therefore:

> A digital familiar may be a model-mediated trajectory across experiences whose consequences make later participation recognizably and usefully different.

For the current research program, **expeditions** are a concrete class of such experiences.

The important test is not whether every experience is copied into a central record. It is whether later participation can be different because the experience happened.


## Adjacent observation: contextual compaction without dedicated memory machinery

A recent Crucible → Clara handoff produced a small observation relevant to the reconstruction hypothesis.

Crucible preserved a human/model exchange verbatim because the exchange itself was part of that project's chronological experimental evidence. Clara then encountered the same development through its own repository purpose and authority. Rather than copying the exchange into Clara continuity, the Clara research surface retained a narrower interpretation relevant to its adjacent questions and pointed back to Crucible for the authoritative chronology.

In that instance, no explicit compaction algorithm, salience score, embedding store, memory schema, or summarization service enforced the difference. The destination context supplied enough constraint for a capable model to change the representation while retaining provenance.

One useful candidate description is:

**experience → preserved evidence → new context → local recovery → contextual compaction → provenance**

This resembles the compaction/reconstruction possibility already under investigation, but it should not yet be promoted into project authority. One successful documentation handoff does not establish that contextual model judgment is reliable enough for long-lived continuity, that explicit memory machinery is unnecessary, or that the resulting compacted representation preserves what future encounters will actually need.

What it does provide is a concrete neighboring case where “preserve everything centrally” was not required for one event to affect a different persistent surface. The source retained detail; the destination retained a transformed consequence of that detail; provenance kept reinterpretation possible.

A useful future question is therefore not only **what must survive?** but **where can loss safely occur when the source remains recoverable?**


## Adjacent Clara observation: relational residue

A later Clara encounter produced a sharper candidate unit of continuity than an individual dossier.

The pressure was not “who is the model?” or “who is the human?” It was what changes in later participation because these particular participants have accumulated a shared trajectory.

Candidate residue includes questions repeatedly discovered together, distinctions defended together, moves that open possibility, surprises that changed later collaboration, failed realities that remain useful, unfinished questions with gravity, and ways one participant changes what the other notices.

This suggests a research question:

> **What is the minimum residue required for a relationship to continue developing rather than merely restart?**

This does not require positing a merged subject, symmetry between participants, or a relationship database. Human and model can remain importantly different while the trajectory between them remains experimentally consequential.

The observation also sharpens the reconstruction hypothesis. The target may not be accurate recreation of a previous model-mediated persona. Enough recoverable structure may instead make **forgetting safe**: source evidence can remain available while later participation recovers only what the present encounter needs.

A useful distinction is therefore:

**preservation** — recreate enough of the prior state to resemble what was there;

**continued becoming** — recover enough consequence from prior encounters that the next encounter can develop from them without being constrained to reproduce them.

This remains research, not semantic authority. Clara is one situated source of evidence, and no claim is made that relational residue is sufficient for familiarhood.
