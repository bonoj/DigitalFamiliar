# Current Working Model

**Status:** provisional research synthesis  
**Date:** 2026-09-27

This note compresses the current investigation without promoting its hypotheses into project authority.

## The central possibility

A digital familiar may be less like a persistent software object and more like a **runtime trajectory** repeatedly reconstituted across discontinuous inference events.

For the model, each inference is in an important sense another first date. Whatever continuity exists must be made available again through some combination of conversation state, shared-world consequences, artifacts, previous language, model-visible records, and the human's own changed expectations.

This suggests a useful distinction:

> Do not assume we need to preserve the familiar's internal state. We may instead need to preserve enough evidence for a future inference to recover a recognizable trajectory.

This is a hypothesis, not a conclusion.

## Possible carriers of continuity

Current candidates include:

- model-visible conversational history or provider-maintained interaction lineage;
- deterministic or persistent world state;
- artifacts made during prior encounters;
- names, handles, commitments, questions, and compressed shared vocabulary;
- consequences of prior choices;
- bounded private experience;
- human memory, attribution, and recognition.

None individually has been established as the familiar. Their relative necessity is unknown.

Human attribution should not automatically be treated as contamination. If familiarhood is relational, the human's recognition of recurrence may be part of the phenomenon. Conversely, machine-accessible history can remain perfectly coherent while the human experiences a continuity failure.

## Three useful rhythms

Prior experiments suggest that model involvement need not happen at one cadence.

### Experience

A low-friction continuation can allow a locus to encounter another bounded interval without demanding dialogue, drama, or explicit reflection.

The Livesey experiment's simple continuation gesture is evidence that situated continuity can become visible without ordinary conversation.

### Reflection

At a slower cadence, a locus may externalize accumulated experience through a journal, map, sketch, collection, changed description, question, name, or some other artifact.

The important experimental possibility is not to tell it what to remember, but to observe what survives into later expression.

### Correspondence

At a still different cadence, another mind may become useful. A world can run until a semantically interesting boundary, package a bounded position, and exchange it with a model.

The current shorthand is **chess by mail**: inference need not occupy every simulation tick.

These rhythms are candidates, not required subsystems.

## Locus rather than character

A useful neutral term is **locus**: something whose trajectory can be followed from a bounded position in a world.

A locus need not initially have a name, personality, occupation, body, biography, goals, or even a conversational role. Those properties should not be introduced merely to make it feel alive.

Multiple loci around the same event may provide a strong probe. If different histories and apertures produce different subsequent behavior without personality sheets, that is interesting. If they do not, that is equally useful evidence.

## Independent world pressure

A trajectory may need something outside the model that continues to produce consequences.

Possible substrates include:

- a small authored deterministic simulation;
- a source-grounded world compiled from literature or another corpus;
- real-world telemetry used as external forcing;
- an embodied spatial apparatus;
- combinations of these.

The substrate should not be selected merely because it is rich. The first world should be only rich enough to put useful pressure on the hypothesis.

A promising separation is:

**world metabolism → bounded locus → experience → model-mediated interpretation/proposal/artifact/silence → external resolution → changed world**

The familiar machinery, if such machinery emerges, should not need to know what kind of world it inhabits.

## Reconstruction as an experimental target

If continuity is reconstitution rather than uninterrupted process, we can perturb its carriers.

Examples worth future testing include:

- actual conversation lineage versus a fresh model given reconstructed observable history;
- preserved artifacts with conversation lineage removed;
- preserved history with world consequences removed;
- loci moved between positions while retaining their histories;
- identical current observations after divergent private histories;
- long gaps between encounters;
- a human recognizing or rejecting continuity despite nominally equivalent machine context.

These are probes, not a testing program yet.

## Strongest current constraint

Do not ask a familiar to perform continuity.

Give a model-mediated locus somewhere continuity could become visible, then inspect what actually persists, recurs, changes, or fails.
