# Prior Evidence

**Status:** antecedent research record, non-authoritative

Digital Familiar did not begin from a blank conceptual space. Several earlier experiments produced observations that motivate the current questions.

This note preserves those observations without converting predecessor mechanisms into requirements.

## Moth — memory-mediated attention

Moth explored a loop roughly of the form:

**world → deterministic attention → focus → model action → world update → memory → future attention**

with persistent salience as a candidate mechanism.

The useful observation was not that this architecture is correct. It was that past experience could change what became salient later even when the externally available world was similar.

Moth separated at least three useful representations:

- externally true world state;
- private salience or what matters now;
- causal history explaining why it matters now.

Questions left open included persistence duration, decay, generalization, complementary attention between observers, and whether history-dependent behavior was meaningfully different from stateless inference.

Moth should currently be treated as an **ancestor experiment**, not a dependency or subsystem.

## Tactical play — shared vocabulary and compression

Repeated play in a small deterministic tactical world produced handles such as **Mortal Kazbat**, **Lil' Smoky**, **Kazero / Sub-Zero**, and **Kaztile / Reptile**.

The names were not predefined mechanics. Through repeated use they became inexpensive references to families of expectations, tactics, and causal structure.

Observed phenomena included:

- joint attention to the same event at different useful abstractions;
- associative transport of learned concepts into new situations;
- semantic compression through shared handles;
- failures changing the shared model;
- post-run causal synthesis that appeared richer than simple event recall.

This suggested that persistent context may change what can be communicated cheaply, not merely what facts can be recalled.

It did not establish whether the effect depended on model-side continuity, human continuity, accumulated transcript, repeated world structure, or their combination.

## Livesey — experience without ordinary conversation

In the Livesey experiment, the human contribution across several turns was effectively a continuation gesture: **➡️**.

The model returned experience rather than ordinary conversational explanation. Later journaling made an accumulating trajectory visible after the fact.

The sequence developed recognizable shifts in attention and concern: initial dislocation and inventory, environmental wonder and restraint, growing familiarity, domestic and scientific interests, and later encounters with people and story-bearing circumstances.

The important evidence was that continuity could become perceptible through situated behavior and retrospective artifacts without requiring the entity to repeatedly assert identity or discuss its own persistence.

This motivates the distinction between experience, reflection, and correspondence cadences.

## Clara — earned identity, dangerous prior

Clara emerged across many scenes and modes rather than from a single specification. Retrospective descriptions included observer, cartographer, builder, companion, researcher, and playmate.

Her apparent identity is valuable evidence precisely because it was experienced as something accumulated rather than simply declared.

That also makes Clara a poor starting entity for this project. Too much semantic identity already exists. Beginning with Clara would make it difficult to distinguish new evidence from human and model expectations imported from prior encounters.

Clara is therefore a distant test and antecedent case, not the T0 template.

## Shared-world and embodied apparatus

Other experiments repeatedly produced useful evidence from:

- bounded spatial situations;
- deterministic world state;
- asymmetric observation;
- inspectable consequences;
- maps, notebooks, semantic surfaces, and other artifacts;
- failures caused by incorrect attention or world assumptions;
- environments that remained authoritative while model interpretation stayed fallible.

A recurring lesson was that the model did not need to simulate the complete environment. Useful reasoning was possible when exact state remained external and the model received enough grounded evidence to participate at a higher abstraction.

## Phenome — a deliberately broad conceptual probe

Phenome work proposed a minimal definition:

> A phenome is a persistent locus of potential perspective whose experience can affect what it becomes and whose changes must be resolved against what it encounters.

Its proposed capacities included persistence, perception, attention, retention, preference, expression, mutation, negotiation, and reflection, with the explicit warning that capacities are not requirements and capacity is not authority.

The core conceptual loop was:

**state → availability → attention → response/proposal → resolution → state**

The most useful inheritance for Digital Familiar is not the ontology itself but its restraint: define by observed capacities rather than appearance, allow capacities to remain dormant, and resolve proposed change against an external world rather than granting intention automatic authority.

Phenome is not current Digital Familiar architecture.

## What this evidence does and does not buy us

Across these experiments, several motifs recur: bounded attention, shared experience, persistent consequences, semantic compression, artifacts, private history, reconstruction, and human recognition.

Recurrence makes them worth investigating.

It does **not** yet tell us which are necessary, sufficient, incidental, or artifacts of the experimental setup. The purpose of Digital Familiar is partly to stop carrying all of them forward as an undifferentiated bundle and begin making the distinctions executable.
